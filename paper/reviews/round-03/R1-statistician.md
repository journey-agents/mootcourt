## Summary (five sentences, in the reviewer's own words, of what the paper claims and shows)
The paper releases a benchmark and protocol for scoring pre-launch forecasts of experiment outcomes against a ledger of concluded tests: a rolling origin over versioned snapshots, a planned-exposure standard error, proper scores with floors and ceilings, a clustered calibration null with the independent one beside it, coverage within strata of realized precision, gates simulated by test and read at each cell's own dependence, a leakage audit, and decision value against an exact random order and against a random order matched on the number of arms. On the headline archive it reports a powered negative: the empirical-Bayes prior gains 0.1493 nats of log score over the base rate and covers 0.8122 and 0.8945 of outcomes pooled, but its Brier skill against the base rate is 0.0015 (minus 0.0018 to 0.0048) on 3,797 tests, its calibration error sits above every one of 500 clustered null draws, and its 90 percent coverage runs from 1.000 to 0.655 across deciles of realized over planned standard error. With the predictive family held fixed, the precedent rung is level with the prior pooled (plus 0.0004 nats paired, minus 0.0003 to 0.0012; Brier skill 0.0019 against 0.0015), which the paper now reads as a cancellation of two measurable movements on the admitted pairs rather than an absence of movement, no ablation moves the paired log score in the rung's favour by more than 0.0009 nats, and the positive control admits a planted one-percent lift in 0.972 of cells at the size of the largest cell while the rung recovers 0.180 (0.164 to 0.197) of the oracle's gain pooled and 0.0995 in the cell shaped like the real one. Ranking by win probability beats the arm-count-matched random order for the text classifier (plus 0.0395, adjusted 0.0158 to 0.0632) and the boosted model, is unresolved for the precedent rung and the local set after adjustment, and the fourth hypothesis is now read on the Brier skill it names, the issued rows at minus 0.0018 (minus 0.0056 to 0.0021) against 0.0019 on all rows. I recomputed every number of my rubric's first item from the per-row score table, and the two extras keys added since round 02 besides, and all of them match the reports to every printed decimal; what remains below is one rubric item added for this round (the hypotheses read in the measure each names), two sentences to sharpen, one caption, and three nits.
