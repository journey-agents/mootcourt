## Summary (five sentences, in the reviewer's own words, of what the paper claims and shows)

The package reads an archive of concluded A/B tests as a ledger of aggregates, forecasts for a proposed test the probability that it ends significant and positive with an interval for the true effect, declines to rule when the ledger has no standing, and scores every forecast out of time with a rolling-origin backtest whose verdicts are gated by a power simulation by test. On the Upworthy archive the Brier skill against the base rate is 0.0014727895194049934 (minus 0.0018181929301026689 to 0.0047984950480917865) for P1 and 0.001898078396287195 (minus 0.001751910789510458 to 0.005366720079675511) for P2 over 3797 tests, and the paired log-score difference of P2 from P1 is 0.0004474073763163793 nats (`tables.aggregate[cell=ALL,rung=P1]` and `[rung=P2]` in `paper/data/run7-tie-rank/backtest-upworthy-20261103-4bfb354b87e3.json`), which a fresh run of tutorial 3 on an exFAT data root reproduced to the last digit in every score, interval and decidability column; on the ASOS archive the README's machine-checked headline block (3224 numbers checked, 0 mismatches by `scripts/verify_readme_numbers.py --reports paper/data/run7-tie-rank`) reads minus 0.1073 (minus 0.2366 to minus 0.0020) with 4 of 4 cells undecidable at this n. What a practitioner receives is the protocol and the tooling: a vendor-neutral ledger format with admission as a status, a backtest report with gates and by-test intervals, frozen cards with a row id, a weekly join that types hits and misses, a reliability command, a one-page report card without the ledger, and an HTTP and MCP service. I followed the README and all six tutorials from a clean clone at a839b2c with no other context, on the internal disk and again with the project folder and data root on an exFAT drive; every command ran, every printed output matched the page after the documentation runner's own mask, and the README card, the tutorial 1 card, the tutorial 2 report card and card and the tutorial 3 archive card matched the pages to the character, ids included, because the pages were regenerated on the day of my run.
