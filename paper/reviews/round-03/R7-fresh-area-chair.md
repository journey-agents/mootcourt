## Summary (five sentences, in the reviewer's own words, of what the paper claims and shows)
The paper releases a benchmark and a protocol for forecasting the outcome of an online experiment before it runs, from the ledger of concluded tests, with a rolling-origin backtest on versioned snapshots, proper scores with floors and ceilings, a clustered calibration null, power-simulated gates and decision value against random order. On the headline archive (3,797 scored tests) the empirical-Bayes prior gains 0.149 nats of log score over the cell base rate yet has a Brier skill of 0.001 (minus 0.002 to 0.005) against a release gate of 250 tests, so the headline result is a powered negative: the ledger gives the distribution of the next result and nothing about which test will be significant. Kernel-weighted precedents held in the prior's family are no better than the prior (plus 0.0004 nats, minus 0.0003 to 0.0012), 33 ablations leave that where it is, and a positive control shows the guard admits a planted one-percent lift at the largest cell's size while the rung recovers 0.18 of it pooled and 0.10 in the cell shaped like the real one. A pairwise TF-IDF classifier captures 3.9 points more positive lift than an arm-count-matched random order at a quarter of the tests (2.2 to 5.4, clear of zero after adjustment), and a first run that scored relative lift at realized precision is reported as a leakage finding. The deployment-shaped archive (78 experiments) is undecidable at every gate, six hypotheses fixed in commit 145eaf7 are read back part by part (one confirmed, four in part, one refuted), and every number traces to a report key that the number audit, the claims audit and this reviewer's recomputation from the per-row score table confirm.
