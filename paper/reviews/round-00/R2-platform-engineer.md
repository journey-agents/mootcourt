## Summary (five sentences, in the reviewer's own words, of what the paper claims and shows)
The paper asks whether a ledger of concluded A/B tests carries out-of-time skill about the next test, and answers on the Upworthy exploratory split that an empirical-Bayes prior is calibrated in coverage and improves the log score over the cell base rate by 0.145 nats, yet has a Brier skill against the base rate of 0.0015 (90 percent interval -0.0018 to 0.0048) at a gate sized for 0.10, and that ranking by predicted win probability captures no more lift than random order. It shows that scoring a ratio metric at the realized precision leaks in-test information (Brier skill -0.107 under the first run) and states a pre-launch protocol with a registered horizon, a planned-exposure standard error, a feature allow-list and calibration by precision stratum that removes it. It wraps the predictors in a ladder with an admission guard that held the precedent rung at the prior in 93 percent of cell-origin pairs, four abstention triggers, a frozen card and a weekly loop with a drift rule, and evaluates the loop by a weekly replay of the archive (13,247 cards, 2,614 abstentions, 49 flagged weeks). The deployment-shaped archive is reported as undecidable at every gate, and a synthetic archive recovers its parameters. The released code regenerates every table from report JSON files, and the backtest's leakage audit reports zero violations on all three archives.

## Findings

Trace of one card (required of R2), on a `moot init` sample project in a scratch folder with the first 100 experiments as history and the last 20 appended later. `moot card` resolves the registry champion (cli.py:105-126), `load_bundle` verifies every artifact digest and refuses an origin before the bundle's fit cutoff (bundle.py:89-95 and 302-335), `build_card` derives s_star from the planned exposure and the cell's median reference rate stored at fit (card.py:284, bundle.py:148-161), reads neighbors visible at the origin only (p2.py:466-506), runs the four triggers (abstain.py:211-254) and `freeze_or_load` writes `cards/<id>.json` once with an exclusive create (card.py:563-625). The card for exp-101 at 2025-07-03 issued with lambda 0, p_sig 0.2087, s_star 0.0150, snapshot 20261103-12617dfc4337, model ea8db7bd4a5c, in 0.8 ms with the bundle loaded. `moot join` at 2025-07-05 appended the 60 incoming rows and attached nothing because the row's usable_after (2025-07-11) was after the join clock (loop.py:112-113); the card file's digest was unchanged. The join at 2025-07-12 wrote the outcome (y -0.031, s 0.016, event false, miss direction_miss, log score 1.921 against P0's 1.911, scored at the frozen s_star) and moved the cell's reliability entry to 2025-07-12 with n 1. The path is leakage-free: no realized quantity reaches the forecast, the horizon guard holds, and the card is never edited. The gaps found on the way are findings 1, 2, 3, 6, 7 and 8 below.

- [blocking] Section 4.4 ("the card abstains for that cell until a join clears it"), Section 6.6 and Section 8; src/mootcourt/loop.py:117-145 and 174-183, src/mootcourt/reliability.py:85-97, scripts/replay_weekly.py:122 and 361. The live weekly join builds the reliability report from issued cards only (an abstained card's outcome carries a null log score and `load_outcomes` drops it), while the replay passes every card's hidden forecast (`forecast=rec["forecast"]`), and the paper does not state the difference. In the live loop this makes abstention absorbing, reproduced on the sample project: (a) a cell whose entry is older than 14 days abstains every card, abstained cards add no evidence, and the entry never refreshes (pages_per_session seeded at 2025-07-02; ten cards at 2025-08-01 abstained "older than 14 days"; five joins to 2025-12-30 attached their outcomes; the entry still reads as_of 2025-07-02, n 7); (b) a drift mark is permanent (checkout_conversion marked drifted at 2025-08-01; ten cards abstained "marked drifted"; the joins attached their outcomes; the entry stays drifted with consecutive_below 2, then ages into staleness). Of the 21 outcomes written, 20 had a null log score. The replay numbers in Section 6.6 (totals.abstained 2,614 of 13,247, n_flagged_weeks 49, and drift onsets for the smallest cell that later clear, at 2013-05-06, 2013-08-12, 2013-11-11, 2014-02-24, 2015-01-19 and 2015-04-13 in replay-upworthy drift_onsets) are produced under a convention the released loop cannot reproduce; the only exit a team has is to re-run `moot backtest`, which reseeds the file with as_of equal to the last usable_after of the scored rows and replaces the drift counters whenever that date is newer (report.py:548-556). The replay script's own note admits that a loop counting issued cards "could never leave an all-abstaining burn-in". Fix: pick one convention and implement it on both paths. Either the frozen card keeps the withheld forecast in a sealed block the join may score for reliability (stated in Section 4.4), or the loop keeps issued-only evidence and gains an explicit recovery rule (a scheduled reseed that the paper says resets drift). In both cases state in Section 6.6 that the replay counted every card's forecast and the live join counts issued cards only, and re-run the replay under the live convention so the reader sees what a team would get.

- [major] Section 4.4 ("Cards are served as a command, a read-only HTTP service and a read-only tool interface"); src/mootcourt/cli.py:128-189, src/mootcourt/service.py:76-86 and 102-113, src/mootcourt/mcp_server.py:35-40, src/mootcourt/loop.py:163-165, docs/tutorials/04-weekly-loop.md:65. No released interface can issue a card that the weekly join can attach an outcome to: `moot card` has no row id option, `CardPayload` has no row id field (a payload carrying `row_id` yields a request whose row_id is None, checked), and the MCP tool never freezes. `attach_outcomes` skips every card without a row id, so the loop of Section 4.4 closes only through the unstable Python call the tutorial resorts to. Fix: add `--row-id` to `moot card` and `row_id` to the HTTP payload, document the row id convention beside the ledger format, and make `moot doctor` count frozen cards that no join can ever score.

- [major] Section 6.6 ("covered 86 percent of joined outcomes with its 90 percent intervals"), Table 5 and Table 9 (cov. 90), Section 6.7; src/mootcourt/card.py:307 against src/mootcourt/harness.py:450-472 and 487, scripts/replay_weekly.py:165-166 and 203, src/mootcourt/loop.py:71. The backtest's coverage is of the predictive of the observed estimate y* (`ystar_intervals` at s_star), while the card's intervals are for the true effect theta (`main.interval(0.9)` with no s_star; the card text says "interval for the true effect"), and the replay's totals.coverage_90_all 0.8598 (n 10,633), the deployment-shaped replay's 0.6579 (n 190) and the live loop's magnitude_miss all compare the observed y with that theta interval. These numbers are therefore not coverage of a forecast of the result and are not comparable with the backtest's coverage_90 0.8945; a theta interval under-covers y by construction, so the reader cannot separate the mechanical shortfall from miscalibration. Fix: carry both intervals on the card (theta for the team, y* at the frozen s_star for scoring), score coverage and magnitude_miss against the y* interval in the join and the replay, and re-run the replay.

- [major] Section 3 (ASOS paragraph, "realized impressions stand in for the planned exposure") and Section 9 ("a correlation of 0.10"); src/mootcourt/scale.py:136-138, src/mootcourt/sources/asos.py:111-132. The exposure proxy is measured on the headline archive only (paper-extras-upworthy impressions_effect_correlation: corr_log_impressions_abs_z 0.0994, corr_log_impressions_event 0.0290, n_rows 13,453) and extended to the deployment-shaped archive by assertion. There the proxy is the user count at the final checkpoint of a run whose length varies from 2 to 131 days (median 43.5) over the 78 experiments; on the 381 admitted rows I measure a correlation of 0.215 between log users per arm and |y/s| and 0.243 between run length and |y/s|, twice the headline figure. On a platform with sequential stopping, which is the common case the paper addresses, the realized count is an output of the stopping rule, so s_star and the log-exposure feature carry the result. Fix: report the correlation per archive in Section 9; add planned exposure or planned duration columns to the exchange format and prefer them in s_star and in the feature when present; for the ASOS run add a sensitivity with s_star from the first checkpoint's count or the experiment's median count.

- [major] Section 3 ("Admission requires at least 1,000 impressions and 10 clicks in both arms"); src/mootcourt/sources/upworthy.py:174-179, src/mootcourt/harness.py:582 and 592. Admission is decided on realized clicks and impressions, so the scored population is conditioned on a post-launch outcome a launching team cannot know: 991 contrasts are low_clicks and 28 low_exposure (DATA.md counts, confirmed on the ledger). Among the 990 low_clicks rows with a computable estimate the significant-negative share is 0.215 and the significant-positive share 0.120, against 0.175 and 0.092 among the 13,453 admitted rows (my computation from the ledger), so the exclusion trims both tails and moves the base rate every skill number is measured against. Fix: say in Section 3 that admission conditions on the outcome and by how much; add a sensitivity run that scores every contrast with a finite standard error (low_clicks admitted, base rate recomputed) and report P1's skill and calibration beside the main run; for a live ledger make the minimum-clicks rule a registered analysis-plan rule, not a filter on results.

- [major] Section 4.4 ("A nightly fit refits every rung on the current snapshot ... promotes a bundle only when its backtest skill beats the champion's by a fixed margin"), Section 8, Appendix D; src/mootcourt/registry.py:159-182 and 198-229, scripts/replay_weekly.py:140-145. The promotion rule treats a refit of the same method on a newer snapshot as a challenger that must beat the champion by 0.02 Brier skill at the gate's test count. On this archive the P2 skill is -0.0078 (-0.0119, -0.0032) and P1's 0.0015 (backtest-upworthy tables.aggregate, cell ALL), so no refit can ever be promoted; the served champion stays at the first fit forever and `moot doctor` warns forever (reproduced on the sample: "not promoted: Brier skill gain 0.0075 is short by 0.0125 of the margin 0.0200; the challenger's backtest has 110 tests, below gate n = 400"; the tutorial calls this "Nothing is broken"). The replay instead refits unconditionally at nine quarterly origins (replay-upworthy header.bundle_origins), so the paper's loop, the replay and what a team gets are three different refit policies: nightly gated, quarterly unconditional, and frozen. The champion-versus-challenger comparison is also unpaired (two backtests on two snapshots and row sets) and carries no interval. Fix: separate "same method, newer snapshot" (replace the champion by default, after a no-regression check on the overlapping rows) from "new method" (the gated promotion, paired on common rows with an interval), and make the replay follow the policy Section 4.4 describes.

- [major] Section 4.4 ("the cell's latest skill at its sample size"); src/mootcourt/card.py:364-368 and 531, src/mootcourt/report.py:535-552, src/mootcourt/reliability.py:98-106. The "Skill at this n" line prints the trailing eight-week Brier skill with a row count and neither an interval nor a gate verdict. On the released Upworthy bundle a card issued at 2015-05-07 prints "Brier skill against P0: 0.075 measured on 265 rows" (reliability-upworthy.json headline_only brier_skill 0.0747, n 265), while the paper's gated figure for the same cell is -0.0043 (-0.0085, -0.0006) over 3,797 tests (Table 1 and tables.aggregate); on the sample project the line read -0.122 on 7 rows. A launching team will read 0.075 as skill. Fix: print the gate verdict and the by-test bootstrap interval of the window, label the window and count tests, and print the latest decidable figure from the backtest beside it.

- [minor] Section 4.4 ("two robustness checks"); src/mootcourt/card.py:230-256, 310-313 and 522-527, src/mootcourt/abstain.py:181-182. When the precedent weight is zero, which is 93 percent of cell-origin pairs (p2-guard-upworthy guarded.share_lambda_zero 0.926), the closed-form p_sig does not depend on the precedents, so the dismissal walk returns None and the instability check returns 1.0 with zero resamples; the card then prints "Precedents to dismiss ... more than 99" and "Share of 0 resamples by test with p_sig on the same side of 0.35: 1.00" (sample project), and an abstained Upworthy card still printed "more than 9" and "Share of 200 resamples ... 1.00". ## Questions for the authors
1. Does any frozen artifact keep the forecast an abstained card withheld? If not, how is the live loop meant to leave the all-abstaining state the replay note says it cannot leave, and after what event does a drifted cell issue again?
2. How is a team meant to refit? A nightly fit, as Section 4.4 says, registers a challenger that a method with skill near zero can never promote; is the intended answer that the team promotes by hand, and if so what does the gate protect?
3. Was the correlation between realized exposure and the absolute standardized effect measured on the deployment-shaped archive, and do the authors know whether those experiments stopped on a rule?
4. Were the outcomes of the low_clicks rows examined before the 10-click rule was fixed, and is the rule meant to be part of the analysis plan a team registers before launch?
5. The reference arm is the earliest-created package and the prior's cell mean is negative (-0.108 percentage points over admitted rows); for a team whose reference is the production control rather than the first-created arm, does the sign convention transfer, and what should such a team enter as headline_changed and image_changed?
6. In the replay the first bundle is fit at 2013-04-01 and cards in the same quarter use history to that date only, while a nightly fit would use history to the previous night; how much of the 2013 drift stretch (Appendix D) is staleness of the quarterly bundle rather than drift?

## What is good (so it is not removed in revision)
- The leakage discipline inside the backtest: `assert_no_leakage` on the frames, the wrapping of every known `fit` so a factory that ignores its rows is still caught (harness.py:96-145), and the independent post-hoc audit that recomputes s_star and the horizon from the ledger (leakage-audit-upworthy.txt and leakage-audit-asos.txt, zero violations on every rung).
- The test-level horizon (method.md call 75) that fixed a real sibling-row exposure of the shared reference arm, and the inclusive visibility rule stated once in ledger.py and applied everywhere.
- The bundle's fit cutoff and `check_origin`, which make it impossible to ask a bundle for a card at an origin before the rows it was fit on; the replay also raises if a bundle's cutoff is after the week.
- s_star frozen on the card and used by the join to score the outcome, so the realized standard error defines only the event (loop.py:117-119); the horizon guard in `attach_outcomes`; write-once outcome files; content-addressed, never-edited cards with an exclusive create.
- The cautionary run reproduced by two flags of the released code and the calibration-by-precision table that would have caught it; the honest treatment of the realized-exposure proxy as a measured leak in Section 9.
- Admission as a status rather than a filter, with every excluded row kept and counted.
- The exchange format's provenance columns, unit checks and the refusal to pool units across a cell; the importers that write their assumptions into `variance_method`.
- The second-opinion protocol with the knowledge-cutoff split and a weight that starts at zero and lapses on a new champion; the MCP tools that never write; the service's single write and loopback default; the boundary sentence on every card and in the health response.
- The plan's six hypotheses reported as they came out, three refuted, and the negative result stated as the contribution rather than hidden.
