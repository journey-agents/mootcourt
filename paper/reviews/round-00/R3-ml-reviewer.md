# Round 00, Reviewer R3: machine learning reviewer
Paper version: f3c8718   Code version: f3c8718

Recommendation: major revision

## Summary
The paper asks whether a ledger of concluded A/B tests predicts the next test's result before launch, and answers with a rolling-origin protocol, a three-rung ladder (cell base rate P0, pooled Student-t empirical-Bayes prior P1, kernel-weighted precedents P2 behind an admission guard), four abstention triggers and a frozen card, evaluated on the Upworthy exploratory split (13,247 scored contrasts, 3,797 tests, nine quarterly origins), the ASOS dataset and a synthetic archive. On Upworthy the prior gains 0.145 nats of log score over the base rate and covers at nominal rates, but its Brier skill on the significant-positive event is 0.0015 (90 percent interval minus 0.0018 to 0.0048), the guard holds the precedent weight at zero in 25 of 27 cell-origin pairs, and the two admitted pairs lose out of time; a gradient-boosted regressor, a local empirical-Bayes comparison set and a TF-IDF logistic classifier run on the same rows and score at or below the prior on Brier skill. A first run that scored relative lift at realized precision is reported as a leakage finding, with a reversed calibration gradient and a Brier skill of minus 0.107. The paper reports the six pre-registered hypotheses as one confirmed, two partly held and three refuted, and releases the code with one command per table. My reading is that the protocol, the leakage finding and the traceability are strong, but the negative result on precedent skill is not yet isolated: the ablations run behind the guard, the challengers are not given the ladder's fallback, no positive control at a plausible signal strength is reported, the feature space never reads the reference headline, and the challengers' decision value, which is computed and omitted, is above random order.

## Findings
- [blocking] Section 6.4, Table 14, abstract, Section 9 (hypotheses that failed). The decision-value table reports random, P0, P1, P2 and oracle only, while the challengers' report computes the same policy for every challenger and the rows are omitted. In `$REPORTS/challengers/backtest-upworthy-20261103-4bfb354b87e3.json tables.decision_value` at origin ALL the captured fractions are random 0.2507, P2 0.2558, GBM 0.2732, local_eb 0.2800, text_lr 0.2859, oracle 0.6824; per origin text_lr is above random at eight of nine origins and tied at the first, local_eb above at eight of nine, GBM at six of nine. The paper states the refutation of H5 without qualification ("prioritizing by predicted win probability captures no more lift than random order") and draws from it the sentence that the ledger says "not which test will win". That sentence is supported for the ladder's own win probability and contradicted, at the three to four point level, by two models the paper itself ran on the same rows. Fix: add the challenger rows to Table 14 with a bootstrap-by-origin or by-test interval on the difference from random order for every policy, restate H5 as refuted for the ladder and open for text-based ranking, and move the text classifier's decision value into the headline argument rather than leaving it out.
- [major] Abstract, Section 1 contribution 3, Section 4.2 ("each a strict generalization of the one below"; "a rung is used only when it beats the rung below out of sample by more than noise"). Both claims hold only at the P1 to P2 step. In the code P0 is Normal(mean(y), var(y)) on the visible estimates, ignoring measurement error (`src/mootcourt/predictors/p0.py`), while P1 is a Student-t prior fit by marginal likelihood with pooling (`p1.py`); no setting of P1 reproduces P0, and the report shows the two are different families (P0 covers 0.9165 and 0.9546 at the 80 and 90 percent levels, P1 0.8122 and 0.8945). There is no admission guard between P0 and P1: at origin 2013-07-01 P1 scored minus 1.727 against P0's minus 1.549 (`tables.score_by_origin`) and was issued anyway; the drift rule in the weekly loop is a later withdrawal, not an admission. Fix: either reword to "P2 nests P1 exactly at lambda zero, and P0 is the scoring baseline outside the ladder", or make P0 a P1 special case (nu infinite, tau squared equal to var(y), no pooling) and add the same guarded out-of-time comparison at the P0 to P1 step, reporting how often it would have held P1 back.
- [major] Section 7, Table 19 (features, bandwidth, selection rule, recency). These ablations run with the admission guard on, so in 25 of 27 cell-origin pairs the setting being ablated never reaches a forecast. The per-cell selection summaries of the features-text, features-structured and main runs are identical (share of lambda zero: both 1.00, headline_only 0.78, image_only 1.00), so the 0.0004-nat spread between minus 0.8961, minus 0.8965 and minus 0.8957 comes from the same two admitted cell-origin pairs. The conclusion "the headline text adds nothing to the structured context, and neither adds anything to the prior" is not isolated by this design; the fixed-lambda rows (minus 0.9662, minus 0.9725, minus 0.9922 at lambda 0.2, 0.5, 1) are the only ablation that measures the precedents themselves, and they are not crossed with the feature sets. Fix: cross feature set and bandwidth with lambda fixed at 0.2 and 0.5 and with the guard off, and add a table, one row per cell-origin pair, of the unguarded selection gain and its clustered standard error (both already in `tables.p2_selection`: for example headline_only 2013-10-01 gain 0.0051, se 0.0017, admitted at lambda 0.8; 2014-10-01 gain 0.0079, se 0.0037, admitted at lambda 0.1) beside the realized out-of-origin P2 minus P1 log-score difference on that pair. That table is the direct evidence that in-selection gains do not transfer; method notes call 66 did it by hand at two origins and no report carries it. It would also let the paper say that two admissions in 27 pairs (7.4 percent) is the guard's own false-admission rate on no-signal data (6 of 72 cells, 8.3 percent, in `paper-extras-synthetic-20261103-bf4fd286e2b2.json guard_admission_rates`), and that both admitted pairs lost out of time (headline_only P2 minus 0.9166 against P1 minus 0.8907), which is the strongest form of the negative result and is not stated.
- [major] Section 4.2, Section 6.2, `src/mootcourt/features.py` `_parse_rows`, `harness.py` `TextLR`. The feature space reads only the variant headline, `image_changed`, `n_variants` and the log planned exposure; `reference_headline` is in the pre-launch allow-list (`scale.PRELAUNCH_FEATURES`) and in every row's `features_json` (`sources/upworthy.py`) and is never used by the feature space, the GBM, local_eb or text_lr. A contrast is a pair, and the two prior text-based winner predictors on this archive that the paper names as nearest neighbours compare the two headlines. A kernel on the variant text alone cannot represent what changed, and in the image_only cell the "text" features are the reference headline shared by every arm. The sentence in Section 6.2 that text_lr's minus 0.0031 is "the shape of result that prior text-based winner predictors on this archive report" also compares a Brier skill on the significant-positive event with pairwise winner accuracy on decisive tests, which are not the same quantity. Fix: add pairwise features (both headlines' SVD vectors and their difference, shared-token share, length difference) to the feature space and a pairwise text_lr, rerun Table 1 and the feature ablation, and report text_lr's pairwise accuracy on tests with a significant winner beside the direction accuracy so the comparison with prior work is like for like.
- [major] Table 1, Section 6.2, `harness.py` `GBMChallenger`, `LocalEB`, `TextLR`. The comparison is on matched splits (verified: P0, P1 and P2 rows are identical in the main and challenger reports, 13,247 rows and 3,797 tests), but it is not on equal terms. The GBM is untuned (max_iter 100, learning rate 0.1, 15 leaves, no time-ordered early stopping), regresses the noisy estimate y without precision weights, and has no calibration layer and no fallback to the prior, while P2 selects among about forty candidates and falls back to P1 whenever the guard holds. Its Brier skill of minus 0.1347 is a calibration failure, not absent discrimination: on the per-row file `challengers/scores-upworthy-20261103-4bfb354b87e3.parquet` I decomposed the Brier score into reliability and resolution over ten bins and found reliability 0.0115 for GBM (P0's is 0.0109) with resolution 0.00023, the largest of any rung (P1 0.00015, P2 0.00017, text_lr 0.00018, local_eb 0.00016; uncertainty 0.0828); within the headline_only cell the AUC of p_sig against the event is text_lr 0.544, local_eb 0.539, GBM 0.527, P1 0.526, P2 0.513. The paper's "every learned challenger loses to P1 or ties it" is true of the proper score and hides that three challengers discriminate a little better than the ladder. Fix: add a discrimination column (AUC or the resolution term) to Table 1 for every rung; give each challenger the ladder's fallback, a guarded stack with P1 at an earned weight or an isotonic or Platt map fit on out-of-fold predictions inside the origin; weight the GBM by 1 over (s squared plus tau squared) and give it a small time-ordered search; then restate what the challengers show.
- [major] Section 6.8, Appendix B, `sources/synthetic.py`. The synthetic archive is run with `text_effect_strength` 0, so it shows specificity (the guard holds lambda at zero in every cell, 6 of 72 false admissions at two standard errors) and the recovery of mu and tau, but never sensitivity. The only evidence that P2 can find a precedent signal is the unit test `tests/test_p2_guard.py::test_a_planted_strong_text_signal_selects_lambda_above_zero` with STRONG = 4.0 against cell spreads of 1.5 to 3.0, a gap of 1.3 to 2.7 prior standard deviations between headlines with and without a signal word, and `tests/test_p2.py` at four standard deviations; nothing of that size is plausible on headline tests. Without a sensitivity curve the paper cannot separate "the precedents carry no gain to recover" from "the guard cannot detect a gain of the size that would matter at these cell sizes". The synthetic noise regime is comparable to the headline archive for the prior (synthetic tau over s of 1 to 2; Upworthy split-half tau 0.25 to 0.42 points against a median s of 0.273, so 0.9 to 1.5), which makes such a curve meaningful. Fix: backtest synthetic ledgers with planted strengths of 0.1, 0.25, 0.5 and 1 prior standard deviations at 600, 2,000 and 7,000 history rows, report the guard's admission rate and the realized P2 minus P1 gain at each point, and state the minimum detectable precedent effect at Upworthy's cell sizes as a number.
- [minor] Section 7 learning curve, Table 4, `learning_curve.py`. The smallest history fraction (0.05) leaves 601 rows, above the 375-row gate, so the curve never crosses the gate and cannot answer the plan's RQ5 (whether the empirical curve matches the gate sizes); the plan's E5 specified 20 seeds and the run used 3 (`seeds 3` in the report) without the paper saying so. The seed spread of P1's skill is 0.006 to 0.008, so three seeds are enough for P1 but not for the GBM (seed spread 0.218 at fraction 0.05). Fix: add fractions 0.01 and 0.02, use the planned 20 seeds, and name the deviation.
- [minor] Section 6.4. P1's win probability is constant within a cell at an origin (x-independent rungs are scored once per cell in `harness._score_rung`), so under the policy of Section 5 a P1 ranking is a ranking of cells with random tie-breaking inside them; the equality with random order on Upworthy follows from the design wherever the cells' mean lifts are close, and the synthetic 0.4453 against 0.2490 is the same mechanism with cells that differ. The results section should say this where H5 is scored, not only in Appendix B, and should say that P2's edge is one origin (2014-10-01, 0.2988 against 0.2508) before the pooled 0.2558 is quoted.
- [minor] Section 6.3. "In the top two [deciles], where it admits precedents most often" reads as row-level admission; lambda is chosen per cell and origin, and the top deciles differ because rows of the two admitted cell-origin pairs fall there. Reword.
- [minor] Appendix B, Table 7. The split-half recovery is stated as "within a few tenths" without the sampling error of the fits; with about 290 rows per half the standard error of a cell mean is roughly tau over the square root of n, 0.1 to 0.2, so gaps of 0.03 to 0.16 are inside noise. Report the recovery against the generator's truth with an interval, not only odd against even.
- [minor] Table 19 caption and Section 6. The ablation runs use 200 bootstrap resamples (`scripts/run_ablations.sh`, `--bootstrap 200`) while the main run uses 500; the lambda-0 row's interval differs from P1's in the fourth decimal for that reason. State the resample count where the intervals differ from the main table.
- [nit] Table 4. The always_reference and always_variant columns are empty by construction; drop them from the learning-curve table.
- [nit] Section 7. "lambda = 0 reproduces P1 to the fourth decimal" understates the code: `P2Predictor.predictive_from_state` returns P1's predictive object when lambda is zero, so the equality is exact, and the sentence can say so.

## Questions for the authors
1. Is the "strict generalization" wording intended to cover P0 to P1, and if so by which setting of P1 is P0 recovered?
2. Were the challengers' decision-value rows seen before H5 was written up as refuted, and is there a reason they were left out of Table 14?
3. At Upworthy's cell sizes (about 7,000 headline_only rows at the last origin), what is the smallest planted precedent effect, in prior standard deviations, that the two-standard-error guard admits with probability 0.9?
4. Why does the feature space omit the reference headline when it is on the allow-list and in every row?
5. Under forward-chaining selection, are the same two cell-origin pairs admitted, and what do they score out of time?
6. Was the GBM's p_sig ever recalibrated through the reliability bins it is passed through, and what is its Brier skill after an isotonic map fit on out-of-fold predictions?

## What is good (so it is not removed in revision)
- Matched splits are real: P0, P1 and P2 rows are identical in the main and challenger reports (same snapshot 20261103-4bfb354b87e3, 13,247 rows, 3,797 tests, nine origins), and the direction baselines sit in the same table.
- Every number I checked against the report JSON matched: P1 minus 0.8812 against P0 minus 1.0259, Brier skill 0.0015 (minus 0.0018, 0.0048), coverage 0.8122 and 0.8945; the 2013-07-01 origin; the challenger skills minus 0.1347, minus 0.0152 and minus 0.0031 (minus 0.0079, 0.0018); the guard comparison 0.926, 0.519 and 0.0403 (0.0327, 0.0481); the lambda, bandwidth, kappa and prior ablations; the learning curve 0.0266 (0.0131, 0.0429) and 0.0295 (0.0141, 0.0492); the decision shares 0.2498, 0.2489, 0.2558, 0.6824 and the synthetic 0.4453; the released-row scores minus 0.954 and 0.453, minus 0.871 and 0.524 now in the paper-extras file; the impressions correlation 0.0994.
- Feature leakage is handled properly: the TF-IDF, SVD, standardization, bandwidth and novelty threshold are fit on visible rows at each origin, the fit methods of P0, P1, P2 and the feature space are wrapped to raise on a not-yet-usable row, hyperparameters are chosen inside the origin by leave-one-test-out, the forward-chaining rule is reported as an ablation, and the post-launch block is refused by the allow-list.
- The guard is the right idea and is measured honestly: the fixed-lambda rows are monotone, the guarded-minus-unguarded paired difference carries an interval, and the no-signal false-admission rate that set the multiplier is reproduced in a report file.
- The cautionary run is a genuine methodological contribution, and reporting the reversed calibration gradient by precision rather than hiding the first run is the right call.
- Pre-registered hypotheses are reported as they came out, three refuted, with the reasons.
- The per-cell-origin selection diagnostics (lambda, unguarded lambda, gain, standard error, guard held) are already in the report, which makes the missing analyses above cheap to add.
- The code is clean, documented at the function level, and tested, including the lambda-zero equality, leave-one-test-out leakage, truncated against exact neighbours and the guard record.
