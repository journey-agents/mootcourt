## Summary
The paper releases a benchmark and protocol for forecasting the outcome of a proposed A/B test from the ledger of concluded tests before launch: a ledger format with admission as a status, a rolling-origin backtest over versioned snapshots with a planned-exposure standard error, proper scores with floors and ceilings, calibration against a clustered null and within precision strata, gates by test at the archive's own base rate, a leakage audit, and decision value with intervals against an exact random baseline. Its baseline suite is a ladder (the cell base rate P0, an empirical-Bayes Student-t prior P1, kernel-weighted precedents P2 behind an admission guard, four abstention triggers and a frozen card) and three recalibrated challengers on matched splits, run on the Upworthy exploratory split (13,247 scored contrasts from 3,797 tests over nine quarterly origins), the ASOS dataset (337 contrasts, 68 experiments) and a synthetic generator with known truth. The headline result is a powered negative: P1 improves the log score over P0 by 0.149 nats (backtest-upworthy tables.aggregate cell=ALL: P1 logscore -0.8566, P0 -1.0059) and covers 81 and 89 percent on average, yet its Brier skill against the base rate is 0.0015 (-0.0018, 0.0048) on 3,797 tests against a release gate of 250 (gates.json by_base_rate 0.09 by_target 0.90 n_400 250), its ECE sits at the 100th percentile of the clustered null, and its 90 percent coverage runs from 1.000 to 0.655 across deciles of realized over planned standard error; P2, held in P1's predictive family, has the same skill and loses 0.004 nats. Ranking by win probability beats random order for every rung whose probability varies within a cell (tables.decision_value origin=ALL budget=0.25 diff_random: text_lr 0.0502 (0.0332, 0.0659), P2 0.0146 (0.0028, 0.0273)) and equals it by construction for P0 and P1, a first run that scored relative lift at realized precision is diagnosed as leakage (Brier skill -0.1074), and ASOS returns undecidable at every gate. Every round-00 blocking and major finding is resolved at this commit; what remains are two captions whose numbers do not trace to any report, a process claim the repository cannot support, one table without the adjusted bounds the protocol promises, and one comparison claim stated more strongly than what was run.

## Findings

- [major] Table 16 caption (sections/appendix.tex, label tab:ablations-upworthy) and the ablations-asos caption (label tab:ablations-asos). The captions state P1 scores that do not trace to any run5 report. The headline caption says "P1 scores minus 0.881 in every row except prior-normal (minus 0.957), admission-prelaunch (minus 0.856, on 14,235 rows) and the three kappa rows (minus 0.882, minus 0.880, minus 0.879)"; the run5 files give P1 logscore -0.8566 in every unchanged row (ablations/backtest-upworthy-20261103-4bfb354b87e3--lambda-0.0.json and the others), -0.9566 for prior-normal, -0.8322 for admission-prelaunch (14,235 rows), and -0.8599, -0.8588, -0.8553 for kappa 0, 5 and 80; Section 7's prose carries the right values (-0.860, -0.859, -0.857, -0.855), so the paper contradicts itself. The deployment-shaped caption says "P1 scores 4.610 in every row except prior-normal (4.332) and the kappa rows (4.589, 4.602, 4.617)"; the files give 4.7088, 4.3318 and 4.6927, 4.7007, 4.7162 (ablations/backtest-asos-20261103-6bb5a15c5ff0--kappa-*.json, --prior-normal.json). These are the run3 values from before the analytic scoring moved every log score by about 0.02 nats, left behind when the tables were regenerated. No conclusion changes, but the protocol's stop condition requires every number in the paper to trace to a report JSON, and these do not. What would fix it: have build_tables.py emit the caption sentence (or a P1 column) from the ablation files, or delete the sentence and let the table carry a P1 row; then grep every caption for digits and check each against its source comment, since captions are the one place the source-comment discipline does not reach.

- [major] Section 9 ("The pre-registered hypotheses"), Section 1 ("The six hypotheses stated before the runs"), Section 5 ("the pooled interval is the pre-registered claim"). The word pre-registered is not supported by the record. The plan (paper/plan.md) entered the repository in commit 145eaf7 of 3 November 2026 together with the first run2 report files (paper/data/run2-prelaunch-clean/gates.json and run-log.json are in the same commit), so there is no timestamp of the hypotheses older than the first results; and the plan's own text (its line 23) describes the result of the first run and the design of the second, so the file was revised after a run. The hypotheses H1 to H6 are unchanged since that commit, which is worth stating, but that is the most the record shows. "Pre-registered" has a specific meaning to the readers of a benchmark track (a public, timestamped registration), and the paper also uses "pre-register" in its archive-norms sense in Sections 3 and 9, so a reader will take the stronger meaning. What would fix it: write "stated in the plan before the runs, by the launching team's own record" and say in one sentence what the record shows (the plan and the first reports share a commit; the hypotheses are unchanged since; the plan was revised after the cautionary run); or, for the next round, register the plan at a public timestamped location before any re-run and cite it. Keep "the pooled interval is the primary claim" in Section 5 in place of "pre-registered".

- [major] Section 5 ("Every table of intervals also carries Bonferroni-adjusted bounds"), Section 6.5, Section 9 (H5 "Confirmed"), abstract ("the precedent rung by 1.5"). Table 8 (tables/decision-intervals-upworthy.tex) is the one table of intervals without adjusted bounds; tables.decision_value rows carry adjusted_lo and adjusted_hi as null. The verdict on H5 for the precedent rung rests on the unadjusted interval, P2 diff_random 0.0146 (0.0028, 0.0273), while Sections 6.2 and 7 decline to read unadjusted excursions as findings. Approximating the bootstrap standard error by the interval width over 2 times 1.645 and applying the paper's own rule (the normal quantile for 1 minus 0.05 over m), with m = 6 rungs at the stated budget of a quarter the P2 bound is about (-0.003, 0.032) and local_eb (0.0238) about (-0.001, 0.049), while text_lr (0.0502) stays at about (0.027, 0.074) and GBM (0.0315) at about (0.013, 0.050); with m = 18 over the three budgets P2 is about (-0.006, 0.035) and local_eb about (-0.005, 0.053). So the paper's own adjustment, applied to this table, would leave the precedent rung and the local set unresolved and the two text-using challengers clear. What would fix it: add the adjusted columns (the harness computes them for every other table), state in Section 6.5 and Section 9 which reading H5 rests on (one pre-registered pooled interval at a quarter, in which case say so and read only that one row unadjusted; or the table-wide rule, in which case H5 is confirmed for the text classifier and the boosted model and not resolved for the precedent rung and the local set), and give the abstract's "the precedent rung by 1.5" its interval or drop it.

- [major] Section 2 ("our pairwise text classifier is that baseline on matched splits"; "The nearest neighbours [li2025learning, ye2025lola, clark2026predict, khimani2026finetuning] are compared on matched splits"), Section 1 ("there the text carries a little"), abstract. What is compared on the matched splits are in-house re-implementations of the simplest member of each family: a TF-IDF logistic regression and a local empirical-Bayes rung "the shape of" li2025learning. None of the published models was run: not the embedding or fine-tuned rankers of ye2025lola, not the retrained 3B model of clark2026predict (52.8 percent on decisive tests against 25.6 random, per related-work.md 2.7), and not the released fine-tune of khimani2026finetuning (0.689 pairwise in domain, 0.843 on pairs from significant tests, per related-work.md 8.13). The paper's text rung reaches a decisive-row direction accuracy of 0.6010 (tables.aggregate rung=text_lr direction_accuracy_decisive) and a recalibrated Brier skill of 0.0046 (0.0003, 0.0087), so the conclusions "the text carries a little" and "precedents equal the prior" are statements about the TF-IDF and SVD feature space, and the ablations vary that space only within TF-IDF. The claim is honest in what it measures and too strong in what it says was compared. What would fix it: at minimum, rewrite the two sentences to "re-implementations of the same family on matched splits; the published models were not run", and say "with TF-IDF features" where the abstract and introduction summarize the text result; better, add one stronger text representation as a fourth challenger on the same origins and rows (a released fine-tuned ranker needs no training, or a sentence-embedding kernel in place of the SVD columns), since a benchmark paper's negative on text is read as a ceiling and the ceiling tried here is low.

## Questions for the authors

P2's precedents lose log score out of time on every admitted pair pooled (paper-extras-upworthy guard_pairs.summary pooled_realized_p2_minus_p1 -0.0067) and yet rank tests better than random (tables.decision_value P2 diff_random 0.0146 at a quarter). 2. Which reading does the H5 verdict rest on: the one pooled interval at a quarter for the issued rung, read unadjusted as the single stated claim, or the table-wide adjusted rule the protocol promises for every table of intervals? Will the decision table carry the adjusted columns in the next build?
3. Is there any record of the hypotheses older than commit 145eaf7, which holds both the plan and the first run2 reports? If not, will the next round's plan be registered at a timestamped location before the re-runs?
4. Was any published text ranker run on the matched split? If the released fine-tune of khimani2026finetuning can be run without training, what is its pairwise accuracy on the decisive rows of this split against text_lr's 0.6010, and does its recalibrated Brier skill clear 0.0046?
5. In the learning curve, is skill scored against the P0 fit on the same subsample (as the Brier columns suggest) or against the full-history P0? If the former, will the table show absolute Brier as well?
6. For the benchmark track: what is the released artifact in one list (schema, digests, admission rules, origins, refused files, harness, report card), where does a new result go, and who maintains the benchmark and names its versions?

## What is good
- The round-00 blocking finding is fixed the right way: the base-rate forecaster is scored as a rung with zero skill by construction, the nesting claim is restricted to the step that nests, and every "against the base rate" names the quantity (Section 4.2; tables.aggregate rung=P0 brier_skill 0.0).
- The cautionary run and its diagnosis are a transferable lesson and now have their figure (Figure 5): a ratio metric against a noisy reference scored at realized precision reverses the calibration gradient (paper-extras cautionary_deciles decile 1 p_sig 0.163 against event 0.0045) and the headline score hides it (brier_skill -0.1074). Keep it as a named contribution.
- The honest arithmetic around the guard: in-selection gains do not transfer out of time (guard_pairs mean_in_selection_gain 0.0062, pooled realized -0.0067), the guard limits noise rather than promising a gain (p2-guard-upworthy difference.logscore -0.0011 (-0.0017, -0.0006)), and the positive control caught a defect in the prior's period level and reported it (guard_period_control old_rule share_admitted 0.389 at 11,100 tests, new_rule 0.000). A paper that shows its own bug and the control arm that found it is doing the benchmark's job.
- Decision value kept apart from calibration, the random share exact by linearity, the structural null for feature-free rungs stated in the table, and the reconciled reading that a calibrated forecaster with no discrimination has zero decision value.
- Coverage by stratum (Figure 1): nominal on average and from 1.000 to 0.655 across deciles of the realized-to-planned ratio, reported against the paper's own interest, with the scale mixture over the level named as not built.
- The planned-event sensitivity (Table 26), which answers the round-00 leakage objection with a number (P1 brier_skill_planned -0.0002 (-0.0037, 0.0038)) rather than an argument.
- The gates at the archive's own base rate with within-test dependence, read as a sizing convention, with the powered claim resting on the interval.
- The related-work section now credits the lineage of every borrowed idea and names the three screening systems against which the narrowed novelty claim is stated; the fetch-verified record in related-work.md is a model for how a benchmark paper should keep its bibliography.
- Snapshot digests, one command per table with the report path at the head of each, source comments on every numeric sentence, committed checksums, the holdout and confirmatory files refused by name, and the deployment-shaped archive reported as undecidable rather than hidden.
- The boundary sentence, stated once and kept: the card prioritizes and sizes, the live test decides.

## Venue
The datasets and benchmarks track remains the right home, and the reframing is done in the words of the abstract, the contributions list and Section 10. Three things stand between the document and that track: the artifact statement of finding 9, the checklist and the single-column style with its page limit, and the four major findings above, none of which needs a backtest re-run (two are text, one adds columns from stored resamples, one is a text fix with an optional extra challenger). CODE@MIT is still the right audience for the talk on the leakage finding and the guard's control arm. The applied data science track of a mining conference remains a weak fit for a protocol with a negative headline and no deployment; a web conference would judge the text result on impact, and the text result is small by the paper's own account.
