## Summary (five sentences, in the reviewer's own words, of what the paper claims and shows)
The paper releases a benchmark and protocol for forecasting the outcome of an A/B test before it launches from a ledger of concluded tests: a rolling-origin backtest over content-addressed snapshots, proper scores with floors and ceilings, a clustered calibration null, by-test gates from a power simulation at the archive's base rate, a leakage audit, and decision value against an exact random baseline, with a ladder of three rungs and three recalibrated challengers as the baseline suite on the Upworthy exploratory split, the ASOS dataset and a synthetic generator. Its headline result is a powered negative: on the headline archive the prior gains 0.149 nats of log score over the base rate (`backtest-upworthy-20261103-4bfb354b87e3.json tables.aggregate[cell=ALL]`, P1 logscore minus 0.8566 against P0 minus 1.0059) while its Brier skill is 0.0015 (minus 0.0018 to 0.0048) on 3,797 tests against a gate of 250 (`gates.json by_base_rate 0.09 by_target 0.90 n_400`), its ECE sits above every draw of the null, and precedents held in the same family equal the prior on skill and lose 0.004 nats. The paper claims that every table and figure regenerates from the report JSON files shipped under `paper/data/run5-period-rule`, that nothing is bundled, that the download refuses the archive's restricted splits, and that the whole pipeline runs in 1,469 seconds on a laptop. From a clean clone, a fresh download and an empty data root, every one of the 3 backtests, the positive control, the gates, both replays, both learning curves, the cautionary run, the three extras files and 45 of the 46 ablations regenerated with zero differing numbers, and all 53 result tables built from that run are byte-identical to the committed ones. What remains is one stale ablation file on the deployment-shaped archive (two printed digits move), two numbers that trace to no shipped JSON, and provenance and packaging items; none of them touches a conclusion.
