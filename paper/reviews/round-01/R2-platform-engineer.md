## Summary (five sentences, in the reviewer's own words, of what the paper claims and shows)
The paper releases a benchmark and protocol for forecasting the outcome of an A/B test before it launches from a ledger of concluded tests, with a visibility rule keyed on each test's registered horizon, a planned-exposure standard error, an allow-list of pre-launch fields, proper scores with floors and ceilings, a clustered calibration null, gates by test and a leakage audit. On the headline archive (3,797 scored tests over nine quarterly origins) the empirical-Bayes prior improves the log score over the cell base rate by 0.149 nats (backtest-upworthy tables.aggregate cell ALL, P1 minus 0.8566 against P0 minus 1.0059) but has a Brier skill of 0.0015 (minus 0.0018 to 0.0048) at a gate of 250 tests, and the precedent rung behind an admission guard equals it (P2 0.0015, minus 0.0017 to 0.0047) while losing 0.004 nats; a positive control shows the guard admits a planted signal. The operational half is a frozen card with a sealed withheld forecast, a weekly join that scores issued and abstained cards alike with the card's own predictive, a drift rule, a champion refresh by newer data and a gated promotion of a different method, exercised by a weekly replay of the archive under the live convention (replay-upworthy totals: 13,247 cards, 1,361 abstentions, 4 flagged weeks, coverage 0.9008 of 11,886 issued outcomes). The deployment-shaped archive is undecidable at every gate and shows the prior overconfident there (Brier skill minus 0.107), and the exposure stand-in is now measured per archive (0.0994 against 0.2152). My trace of a card from issue to outcome reproduces the join's scores from the frozen file alone; the findings below are about what the released loop does not enforce, namely that a scored card predates its test, that one row yields one scored card, and that the nightly fit sees only rows usable at the fit time.

- [blocking] Section 4.3 ("The card is issued before launch and frozen"; "a weekly join attaches outcomes to cards whose horizon has passed"), Section 9 (misuse paragraph); src/mootcourt/loop.py:128-129 and 201-224, src/mootcourt/card.py:342-343. The join's only time check is that the row's usable_after has passed at the join clock; nothing compares the card's origin or issue time with the row's started_at or usable_after. A card issued after the result is in the ledger, from a champion fit on that result, is therefore joined and enters the reliability report as evidence. Reproduced: after the refresh, the champion 6e5029e03c73 had fit_cutoff 2025-12-29 on 360 rows including exp-120 (usable 2025-12-29); `moot card --row-id sample:exp-120:treatment::checkout_conversion --now 2025-12-30` was accepted (it abstained for staleness, so its withheld forecast was scored), and the join at 2025-12-31 wrote an outcome typed hit, at 2025-12-29, which became the cell's window (n 1, Brier skill 0.177). The outcome file carries no sign of this; the drift rule and the card's "Skill at this n" line read it as a pre-launch forecast. The replay cannot produce this case because it issues every card at the week start before started_at, so no paper number is affected, but the released loop's record, which the paper offers to a launching team as the thing that makes a card worth something, can be filled after the fact with no trace. Fix: in `outcome_from_card`, refuse an outcome when the card's origin or issued_at is at or after the row's usable_after, and mark `in_flight: true` when it is at or after started_at (the tutorial's own exp-101 card is such a card, issued 2025-07-03 for a test that started 2025-06-27); exclude both from `reliability_row`, count both in `moot doctor`, write the rule into Section 4.3 and the operations manual, and add a test that a card issued the day after a result is never scored.

- [major] Section 4.3 (the frozen card "with the identifiers of the snapshot, model and feature set"); src/mootcourt/card.py:342-343, src/mootcourt/service.py:76-79, src/mootcourt/cli.py:171-180. A card may stand at an origin later than its issue time: `moot card --now 2025-12-01 --origin 2025-12-30 --row-id sample:exp-119:...` froze a card whose issued_at precedes the test's launch while its origin, the time whose rows it saw, follows the result. No interface checks origin <= now, and the issue time itself is caller-supplied on every interface (`--now`, the service clock), so the frozen timestamp is not evidence that the forecast predated the result. Fix: refuse origin after the issue time on the command, the service and the tool; record the system clock on the card beside the stated issue time (`recorded_at`, inside the content id); and say in Section 4.3 that the registered times are the team's statements and the join's guard (finding 1) is what enforces them.

- [major] Section 4.3 and Section 6.6 ("one card per admitted row"); src/mootcourt/loop.py:213-223, src/mootcourt/reliability.py:125-145, src/mootcourt/service.py:165-177. Every frozen card with a row id is scored, so two cards for one row give two outcomes and the row counts twice in the rolling scores. Reproduced: cards for exp-103 at 2025-07-10 and 2025-07-11 (different issued_at, different ids) both received the outcome at the 2025-07-30 join, and the cell's entry read n 2, n_tests 1, rolling log score minus 1.8168 (both copies). `freeze_or_load` makes a byte-identical card idempotent, but a repeated `POST /card` carries a new issue time each call, so every retry, refresh of a screen or edited headline for the same test adds a card the join will score; the by-test bootstrap absorbs duplicates within a test only partly, and the rolling log score, the drift decision and the point skill do not. The replay issues exactly one card per row, so its numbers do not have this. Fix: score one card per row, the earliest one that passes the guard of finding 1, write `superseded_by` on the others and leave them out of `reliability_row`; count duplicates in `moot doctor`; state in Section 6.6 that the replay's one-card-per-row convention is what the loop enforces.

- [major] Section 4.3 ("a nightly fit writes a versioned, hashed bundle"), docs/operations.md (Nightly fit); src/mootcourt/registry.py:358-377 (`fit_command`), src/mootcourt/bundle.py:240-248. `fit_command` calls `fit_bundle` with `as_of` unset, so the nightly fit takes every admitted row of the snapshot, not the rows usable at the fit time. The exchange format and `moot ingest --usable-delay-days` exist precisely so that a row can sit in the ledger before it is usable. Reproduced in a second scratch project: the sample ingested with a 600-day delay held 360 rows of which 261 were usable at the wall clock; `moot fit` fit all 360 and recorded fit_cutoff 2027-08-21, after which every card at the wall clock was refused ("origin is earlier than the fit cutoff"). The failure is loud, which is to the code's credit, but the bundle itself was fit on rows not yet usable, `data_through` is a date in the future, and with `--origin` after the cutoff (finding 2) a card issues from it. Fix: `fit_command` passes `as_of` equal to the fit clock, records it in the manifest, and `data_through` becomes the latest usable_after at or before that clock; Section 4.3 then reads "on the rows usable at the fit time".

## Questions for the authors
1. Is a card issued during a test, after launch but before its horizon, meant to be scored like a pre-launch one? The tutorial's exp-101 is such a card, and the replay never issues one.
2. In round 00 eleven of the latency script's thirty made-up headlines abstained by novelty; card-latency.json now records statuses issue 30 while the archive's novelty rate rose from 2.9 to 7.3 percent. Does a request without a reference headline take the fit-mean reference vector (call 187) and so sit nearer the center of the pairwise space than any real pair, and would a team that omits the reference headline see fewer novelty abstentions for that reason?
3. The released reliability-upworthy.json carries consecutive_below 1 for the `both` cell (rolling log score minus 1.6799 against P0 minus 1.3337 on 23 rows, 12 tests), one join short of a drift mark, which matches the replay's onset at 2015-04-20. Is that the intended starting state of the released bundle, and does the seed's `prev=None` build (never more than one consecutive join) mean the first live join can mark a cell the replay had already flagged?
4. Is `as_of` deliberately unset in `fit_command`, and if so what does `data_through` mean for a ledger with a usable delay?
5. On an archive where no rung reaches 0.02, what is a launching team expected to do with `moot promote`, and is the margin meant to depend on the base rate as the gates do?

## What is good (so it is not removed in revision)
- One code path for the join and the replay (`outcome_from_card`, `reliability_row`), verified end to end: the outcome's log score and its P0 baseline reproduce from the frozen card alone, at the frozen s_star, with the realized standard error defining only the event.
- The withheld block, which makes abstention recover in one horizon (traced on a stale cell) and lets the replay and the loop share a convention; the replay totals reconcile (1,361 abstentions with triggers novelty 976, reliability 106, support 304 overlapping; 11,886 issued outcomes equal 13,247 cards less 1,361; miss counts sum to 13,247; coverage 0.9008 beside the backtest's 0.9004).
- The row id on all three interfaces, the refusal of a foreign-source id, the exclusive create, read-only card and outcome files, and digests unchanged across joins.
- The champion refresh as built: a same-method fit on newer data is pending until its backtest, the backtest decides it, the fit time only breaks ties, older data never takes the champion back, and every route writes its reason into the history.
- `check_origin`, which refused every card from the bundle fit on rows not yet usable; the loud failure is what made finding 4 visible.
- The skill line's window interval, test count and gate verdict read at the cell's base rate, and the "Not applicable" robustness lines at lambda zero.
- The admission ablation on exposure only, the exposure correlation computed per archive with the run length, and the latency timed with the precedent weight forced to 0.5.
- The honest treatment of the deployment-shaped archive (undecidable, overconfident, nominal calendar) and of the replay's four flagged weeks in the smallest cell.
- The leakage discipline of the backtest itself: the wrapped fits, the post-hoc audit, the test-level horizon and the inclusive visibility rule stated once in ledger.py.
