## Summary (five sentences, in the reviewer's own words, of what the paper claims and shows)
The paper releases a benchmark and protocol for scoring pre-launch forecasts of A/B test outcomes against a ledger of concluded tests, with a rolling origin, a planned-exposure standard error, proper scores with floors and ceilings, a clustered calibration null, coverage within strata of realized precision, gates by test at the archive's base rate, a leakage audit and a decision-value measure with intervals. On the headline archive it reports a powered negative: the empirical-Bayes prior improves the log score over the base rate by 0.149 nats and covers at nominal rates on average, but its Brier skill against the base rate is 0.0015 (minus 0.0018 to 0.0048) on 3,797 tests, its probabilities sit above every draw of the calibration null, and its 90 percent coverage runs from 1.00 to 0.66 across deciles of realized over planned standard error. With the predictive family now held fixed, the precedent rung equals the prior on Brier skill and loses 0.004 nats of log score, every ablation leaves that unchanged, and a positive control shows the admission guard admits a planted text signal at the size of the largest cell after a defect in the prior's period level was found and fixed. Ranking by predicted win probability is reported to capture more positive lift than random order for every rung whose probability varies within a cell, the text classifier by 5.0 points, and the deployment-shaped archive returns undecidable. I recomputed the pooled log scores, the paired P1 minus P0 and P2 minus P1 differences, the Brier skill with both an independent by-test bootstrap and the package's own seed-0 draws, the Bonferroni bounds, the coverage, the two ECE estimators, the design effect, the latent correlation and the clustered null, all ten coverage deciles and the decision-value rows from the per-row score table and matched the report to four decimals; the findings below are about what two secondary analyses are compared against, not about the arithmetic, and every round-00 answer holds at this commit except as noted.

## Findings

- [blocking] Abstract, Section 6.4 (Table 8, Figure 2), Section 8, Section 9 (H5), `src/mootcourt/decision_value.py:36-47` and `:118-123`. The decision-value comparison is dominated by the number of arms, a pre-launch field, and its baseline does not control for it. A test's value is the largest positive observed lift among its contrasts and its score is the largest `p_win` among them, so both rise mechanically with the number of contrasts: from the parquet (rung P1 rows, 3,799 tests over nine origins) the mean value is 0.096 for tests with one contrast (177 tests), 0.226 with two (306), 0.390 with three (1,880), 0.338 with four, 0.389 with five and 0.452 with six or more, and the text classifier's largest `p_win` per test runs 0.413, 0.462, 0.501, 0.497, 0.499, 0.509 over the same groups (its mean `p_win` 0.413 to 0.440). A policy that ranks tests by their number of contrasts and reads no forecast captures 0.3026 of the positive lift at a quarter of the tests, plus 0.052 over random order (90 percent interval by test within origin, 500 resamples, 0.041 to 0.064), more than the text classifier's 0.3005 (plus 0.0502, 0.0332 to 0.0659 in `tables.decision_value[origin=ALL,budget=0.25]`, which I reproduce). Against a random baseline matched on the chosen set's arm-count composition (each stratum's mean value times the number chosen from it, resampled the same way) the differences become: text classifier plus 0.0395 (0.0215 to 0.0534), boosted model plus 0.0289 (0.0154 to 0.0399), local comparison set plus 0.0150 (minus 0.0006 to 0.0306), precedent rung plus 0.0092 (minus 0.0027 to 0.0198), P1 minus 0.0045 (minus 0.0127 to 0.0046). The abstract's "captures more positive lift than random order for every rung whose probability varies within a cell (... the precedent rung by 1.5)", Section 6.4's "every rung whose probability varies within a cell captures more", and H5 "confirmed ... plus 0.015 for the precedent rung" rest on the unmatched baseline; the precedent rung's unadjusted interval (0.0028 to 0.0273) also does not survive a Bonferroni family of the 15 pooled differences in its own table (z 2.39 on a standard error of about 0.0074 spans zero), and the table carries no adjusted bounds. What would fix it: add the arm-count policy as a named baseline row in Table 8 and the figure; report each rung's difference from the arm-count-matched random baseline (or define a test's score and value per contrast, for example the mean over contrasts, so that the max-of-k structure disappears) with the same by-test intervals and adjusted bounds; reword the abstract, Section 6.4, Section 8 and H5 to the rungs that clear the matched baseline (the text classifier at about 4 points and the boosted model), and say that the precedent rung does not. The text classifier's claim survives at a smaller size; the precedent rung's does not.

- [blocking] Section 7 (Learning curve, Table 23), Section 8 ("a prior that arrives quickly"), `src/mootcourt/learning_curve.py:93-113` and `harness.py:864`. The learning curve's Brier skill is measured against a base rate re-estimated from each subsample, so the baseline worsens as the history shrinks and the curve reads backwards. From `learning-curve-upworthy-20261103-4bfb354b87e3.json` (rung P1) the mean Brier score is 0.05544, 0.05443, 0.05421, 0.05397, 0.05390, 0.05389, 0.05385 at fractions 0.01 to 1.0, monotone in the history, while the reported skill is 0.0679, 0.0366, 0.0352, 0.0316, 0.0300, 0.0307, 0.0295; the implied base Brier score (brier over one minus skill) is 0.05948 at 123 history rows against 0.05548 at 11,997, because `rolling_origin(sub, ...)` computes `p_base` from the subsample's visible rows and a cell base rate from a few dozen rows is a worse forecaster than one from thousands. Against the full-history base rate P1's skill at each fraction is 0.0007, 0.0191, 0.0229, 0.0273, 0.0286, 0.0288, 0.0295: a learning curve that rises with history and plateaus between 1,200 and 3,000 rows. The sentences "is higher, not lower, with 123 rows (0.068, 0.046 to 0.094): more history does not add discrimination to a prior that has learned a base rate and a spread" are therefore not supported; the seed standard deviation of 0.0887 at that fraction (`skill_seed_sd`) also says a team with 123 rows gets one draw from a spread far wider than the interval of the seed-averaged skill. Two further things the section should say: the held-out origin (January 2015, 1,143 rows in 318 tests) is the quarter where P1's skill is highest (`tables.score_by_year` 2015: 0.0297 against a pooled 0.0015), so the curve's level is that quarter's and not the archive's, and P0's row of zeros is by construction and carries no information about how much history a base rate needs. What would fix it: score every fraction against the base rate of the full visible history at the final origin (or report the raw Brier score beside the skill), report the seed spread beside the by-test interval, rerun the curve, and rewrite the paragraph and the discussion sentence to what the fixed-baseline curve shows.

- [major] Section 7 (Learning curve), Table 23 caption and `learning_curve.py:12-15`. The gates are read against the size of the fit history, which is not what they measure. A gate size is the number of scored tests at which a simulated calibrated forecaster of a given Brier skill clears a 90 percent by-test interval with power 0.90 (Section 5); the learning curve scores the same 318 held-out tests at every fraction, so every point of the curve is decidable at both gates (318 exceeds 250) and none of them is "below both gates". The sentence "The two smallest histories, 123 rows in about 35 tests and 244 rows, are below both gates, 605 rows (about 173 tests) sits at the gate for a skill of 0.20 and 1,204 rows (about 344 tests) clears the gate of 250 for a skill of 0.10, so the curve crosses both gates" compares a fit-history count with a scored-test count. How much history a prior needs is a different question with a different answer (the fixed-baseline curve above), and the gates say nothing about it. What would fix it: delete the sentence and the gate lines on the learning-curve figure, state that the held-out set of 318 tests clears both gates so each point is decidable, and if a "history needed" number is wanted, read it off the fixed-baseline curve.

## Questions for the authors
1. Decision value: at budgets of a tenth and a half, what does the text classifier capture against the arm-count-matched random baseline, and is the one-contrast population (value 0.096 against 0.390 for three contrasts) a different kind of test, for example a different period or kind of change?
2. Learning curve: will the rerun score every fraction against the base rate of the full history, and at what history size does P1's Brier score come within 0.0005 of its full-history value (the table above puts it between 1,204 and 2,991 rows)?
3. The event rate falls from 0.118 to 0.057 by origin year while the planned exposure, hence the power of each test, is not falling in the same way (Table calibration-upworthy shows the event rate rising with exposure). Did the publisher's effects shrink, and does P1's 2015 skill come from its prior scale tracking that where the trailing base rate cannot?
4. Of the nine admitted pairs, the two largest in-selection gains (0.0084 at July 2014 and 0.0136 at October 2014, both headline-only) have the two most negative realized differences (minus 0.0267 and minus 0.0153, `guard_pairs.rows`), and forward-chaining selection puts P2 0.0004 nats above P1 (`--p2-selection-forward.json`). Is there a within-quarter change in the second half of 2014 that leave-one-test-out validation cannot see, and why is leave-one-test-out rather than forward chaining the default when the deployment is forward?
5. Will the gate read carry each cell's own rows per test, or will Table gates say which cells it applies to?

- The six hypotheses are still reported as they came out, and the paper gains from it; after the two findings above, H5 will read "confirmed for the text classifier and the boosted model, not for the precedent rung", which is also a result worth having.
