## Summary (five sentences, in the reviewer's own words, of what the paper claims and shows)
The paper releases a benchmark and protocol for forecasting the outcome of an A/B test before it launches from a ledger of concluded tests: a rolling-origin backtest over content-addressed snapshots, proper scores with floors and ceilings, a clustered calibration null, by-test gates from a power simulation at the archive's base rate, a shipped leakage audit and decision value against an exact random order matched on the number of arms, with a ladder of three rungs and three recalibrated challengers as the baseline suite on the Upworthy exploratory split, the ASOS dataset and a synthetic generator. Its headline result is a powered negative: on the headline archive the prior scores minus 0.8566 nats against the base rate's minus 1.0059 (`backtest-upworthy-20261103-4bfb354b87e3.json tables.aggregate[cell=ALL]`, 0.149 nats better) while its Brier skill is 0.001473 (minus 0.001818 to 0.004798) on 3,797 tests against a gate of 250 (`gates.json by_base_rate 0.09 by_target 0.90 n_400`), its calibration error sits at the 100th percentile of the clustered null, its coverage runs from 1.000 to 0.655 across deciles of realized over planned standard error, and precedents held in the same family sit at plus 0.000447 nats (minus 0.000297 to 0.001191) on the paired rows. A text classifier on TF-IDF features captures 0.039459 (0.021737 to 0.054063) more of the positive lift than the arm-count-matched random order at a quarter of the tests, the guard admits a planted one-percent lift in 0.972 of cells at the size of the largest cell and the rung recovers 0.180 (0.164 to 0.197) of the oracle's gain, and the deployment-shaped archive is "undecidable at this n" at every gate. The paper claims that every number traces to a report under `paper/data/run7-tie-rank`, produced by one code state in one run, that every table and figure regenerates from those reports, that the download refuses the archive's restricted splits, and that the whole pipeline runs in 2,024 seconds on a laptop. From a clean clone at this commit, a fresh download and an empty data root, I regenerated all 69 report files of the run folder (the three backtests with their challengers, 49 ablation and guard files, the positive control and its two planted-ledger backtests, the gates, both replays, both learning curves, the three extras files, the three leakage audits) with zero of 609,116 numbers differing at 1e-9, built 61 of the 63 generated tables byte-identical from that run (the wall-time table differs in its timings, as it must, and one table differs only in the sign of seven printed zeros), and found that every previous finding of mine is closed.
