## Summary (five sentences, in the reviewer's own words, of what the paper claims and shows)
The paper releases a benchmark and protocol for scoring pre-launch forecasts of experiment outcomes against a ledger of concluded tests: a rolling origin over versioned snapshots, a planned-exposure standard error, proper scores with floors and ceilings, a clustered calibration null with the independent one beside it, coverage within strata of realized precision, gates simulated by test at the archive's base rate and read at each cell's own dependence, a leakage audit, and decision value against an exact random order and against a random order matched on the number of arms. On the headline archive it reports a powered negative: the empirical-Bayes prior improves the log score over the base rate by 0.1493 nats and covers 0.8122 and 0.8945 of outcomes pooled, but its Brier skill against the base rate is 0.0015 (minus 0.0018 to 0.0048) on 3,797 tests, its calibration error sits above every one of 500 clustered null draws, and its 90 percent coverage runs from 1.000 to 0.655 across deciles of realized over planned standard error. With the predictive family held fixed and forward chaining as the selection rule, the precedent rung is level with the prior (plus 0.0004 nats paired, minus 0.0003 to 0.0012; Brier skill 0.0019 against 0.0015), every ablation stays within 0.013 nats of it, and a positive control shows the guard admits a planted one-percent lift in 0.972 of cells at the size of the largest cell while the rung recovers 0.180 (0.164 to 0.197) of the oracle's gain. Ranking by win probability beats a random order matched on arm count for the text classifier (plus 0.0395, 0.0217 to 0.0541, adjusted 0.0158 to 0.0632) and the boosted model, is unresolved for the precedent rung and the local set after adjustment, and the prior sits at that baseline by construction. I recomputed every headline number of this review's rubric from the per-row score table and matched the report to every printed decimal and beyond, so my findings this round are about what the tables print and how two sentences read the files, not about the arithmetic.

The working tree at 9f725b1 carries two uncommitted one-line edits (the run 7 README's count of moved percentile leaves and the command line recorded in paper/reviews/claims-audit.md); neither touches a number I checked.
