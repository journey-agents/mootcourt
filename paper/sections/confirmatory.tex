Every result outside this section comes from one split of the headline archive that we read many times. The archive's maintainers ask that an analysis of its second, confirmatory split be registered first. On 14 November 2026 we registered three primary readings, each with a rule that turns its numbers into a verdict\iftmlr{} (a dated record, withheld for review)\else{} (commit afad501, pushed to the public repository that day)\fi. The file was fetched on 15 November 2026 and analysed once. The package under \texttt{src} did not change in between, and three scripts were added: the guarded fetch and the script for the smoothed base rate before the fetch, and the script that runs the registered stages in order just after it (the end of this section). The registration is our own dated record: no outside registry held it before the analysis, and no journal reviewed it. On 17 November 2026, after the analysis, we deposited its text with the OSF registry as a retrospective registration\iftmlr{} (link withheld for review)\else{} (\url{https://osf.io/jf5ds})\fi. That deposit makes the text easier to find and adds no evidence of its earlier date. Its three readings are not the six hypotheses of Appendix~\ref{sec:verdicts}, which were read on the exploratory split only.

The paper therefore claims the following as tested by a registered analysis, on further tests of the same seven quarters: the prior's lead over a heavy-tailed base rate is not shown with the quarters as the units, its skill against a smoothed base rate is not shown, and the text classifier is not shown to rank better than counting arms. Only the second reading is adjusted for three, as registered, and adjusting the first and third could not change a verdict whose interval already contains zero. With tests as the units and the quarters held fixed, the lead and the adjusted skill are clear of zero on this split. The registered rules read the seven quarterly values, which further tests of the same quarters cannot multiply. The registration said that a null on all three would be reported as the paper's main finding. All three are null. We depart from that wording in one respect: we report the three verdicts as the paper's only confirmatory result, placed last in the abstract, and keep the near tie of the base rate and the prior as the first finding. The first verdict is consistent with that near tie and does not establish it. Every other result, the secondary readings above included, remains exploratory.
