In the main run, its unadjusted interval sits above zero in 2013 and in 2015 and below zero in 2014, and each adjusted interval spans zero. When the randomization window is included, the 2015 interval alone stays on its side. Year by year, the rate of significant positive results declines. The trailing base rate of P0 falls behind that decline, since it is the rate computed over rows that are already visible. The drift rule is not designed to catch it either. The rule looks only at the gap between the rolling log score of a cell and that of P0, and a decline in the rate that the two forecasts both miss need not shift that gap.

The backtest looks at the reliability report of each cell once per quarter, at the origin. In use, the loop of the package operates weekly (Section~\ref{sec:method}). The weekly replay (Table~\ref{tab:replay}, Appendix~\ref{app:tables}) takes that loop and runs it across the archive under rules of its own. For each scored row, that is, each admitted row with a test that begins at or after the first origin, it issues one card at the opening of the week in which the test begins, taken from the bundle fitted at the most recent quarterly origin on or before that week. Each outcome is joined at the first weekly join on or after the date when the result could be used, which on this archive is the assigned end of the test (Section~\ref{sec:setting}), it is scored using the card's own predictive (an abstained card is scored on the forecast it withheld), and the drift rule is applied at every join.

The prior is not alone in showing either the calibration shortfall or the low coverage of the 80 percent interval. The base rate fits no spread, and its calibration error sits at percentile 99.8 of the same null. The 80 percent interval of the base rate is a pair of empirical quantiles and it covers 78 percent, just as the prior's interval does. The cause is therefore more likely to be the scored rows of this single ledger, or a null that rejects too often, than the prior's fit, and we have not pulled those two apart: no report states how frequently the clustered null rejects a forecaster that is calibrated by construction. The control gives no support to a claim of exact calibration. In the headline archive the calibration error exceeds every draw of the null, so that particular rejection does not hinge on this margin.

% src: $RUN6/positive-control/backtest-synthetic-20261106-8cc34d3af720.json header.admitted_rows 4000 (text_effect_strength 1.0, a third of the headline_only prior sd of 3.0; src/mootcourt/sources/synthetic.py theta adds strength times (signal minus 0.5)); $RUN6/paper-extras-synthetic-20261103-bf4fd286e2b2.json positive_control_sensitivity.rows strength_over_prior_sd headline_only 0.333; positive_control_sensitivity.inputs held_out_share_of_tests 0.15, seeds 0 to 11 (twelve), share_admitted (selected lambda above zero in a cell), oracle_gain (P1 refit with the planted term removed from y, its location moved back by the row's term); positive_control_rungs.inputs share_pairs_admitted, oracle; positive_control_sensitivity.rows strength_over_prior_sd both 0.5, image_only 0.667; inputs.held_out (a random share of the tests), share_admitted (P2 fit on the whole ledger) against share_admitted_fit_split, lower at every size at text_effect_strength 1.0 (0.000, 0.111, 0.889 against 0.056, 0.194, 0.972); docs/method.md call 177 (the lift of 1.0 percent fixed when the control was designed, from the headline archive's split-half fits); main.tex H1 names the headline\_only cell
In the positive control a text lift of 1.0 percent is planted between headlines that carry a signal word and headlines that do not, and this amounts to a third of the prior standard deviation in the headline-only cell. The planted signal appears in every cell of the ledger, and it makes up a bigger share of the spread in the other two cells, one half and two thirds, because the spreads in those cells are smaller. The headline-only cell is discussed separately below because hypothesis H1 names it and because its scored rows are equal in number to the scored rows of the real cell. When the control was designed the lift size was set, before any first results of the control existed and after results from the headline archive had been seen, and Table~\ref{tab:positive-control-gain} (Appendix~\ref{app:tables}) lists all the sizes that were run. Two designs are used for the control, and Table~\ref{tab:fmt-control} shows them separately (from Tables~\ref{tab:positive-control}, \ref{tab:positive-control-gain} and~\ref{tab:positive-control-rungs}, Appendix~\ref{app:tables}). The split at the end of the ledger takes a random 15 percent of the tests of a ledger as held out, fits on the remainder as the ledger stands at its end, and does this again over twelve ledgers that were drawn under different seeds. Both its admission column and its gains are taken from the fit that leaves out the held-out tests. With 300 tests, that fit admits nothing in any cell, and for this reason P2 is P1 on every row that was held out, so the zero share there follows by construction. The real archive receives the rolling backtest design. The guard \emph{admits} a cell, or a cell together with an origin, when the precedent weight that it chooses there is above zero. Every recovered figure is expressed as a fraction of the gain in log score of an oracle over P1, and not as a fraction of the lift. The oracle takes P1, fits it with the planted term taken away from each outcome, and then adds that term back into the forecast for each row, so the signal is known to it exactly.

This is not evidence that the classifier's ranking on the real archive is spurious, and one exploratory run on one archive is all the evidence for that ranking.
