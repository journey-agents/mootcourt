The skill of P1 follows no trend across the three calendar years, and the drift rule is not built to catch the decline in the event rate that those years show. Table~\ref{tab:fmt-drift-year} summarises Table~\ref{tab:drift} (Appendix~\ref{app:tables}) grouped by the calendar year in which the test began. In the main run, its unadjusted interval sits above zero in each of the three years, and each adjusted interval spans zero. The one pair of origin and cell whose base rate is zero lies in 2013, so the interval of that year measures that unsmoothed opponent too, and the reading without the pair was made pooled only (Section~\ref{sec:ladder-results}). The same holds in the window ablation. Year by year, the rate of significant positive results declines. The trailing base rate of P0 lags that decline, because it is the rate over rows that are already visible. The drift rule is not designed to catch the decline either. The rule looks only at the gap between the rolling log score of a cell and that of P0, and a decline in the rate that the two forecasts both miss need not shift that gap.

On an archive whose truth is known, the fitted prior returns the generator's cell means and spreads, and its calibration is neither confirmed nor clearly rejected. The synthetic archive (Appendix~\ref{app:synthetic}) contains three cells whose means and spreads in percent lift are known, two variants for each test alongside a reference, and no signal in the text, and it scores 907 tests across eight origins. The generator draws effects, spreads and standard errors in a single unit, which it calls percent lift. On this archive we fit the prior to each cell on its own, without pooling across cells, twice: on the odd-numbered tests and on the even-numbered tests. Both halves get the generator's cell means back to within two tenths of that unit (Table~\ref{tab:fmt-app-syn-truth}), and the guard keeps the precedent weight at zero everywhere. Stated as standard deviations, the fitted spreads of both halves come within three tenths of the generator's in every cell; the split-half report records the degrees of freedom of each fit, which are needed to turn a fitted Student-t scale into a standard deviation. The prior is not alone in showing either the calibration shortfall or the low coverage of the 80 percent interval. The base rate fits no spread, and its calibration error sits at percentile 99.8 of the same null. The 80 percent interval of the base rate is a pair of empirical quantiles and it covers 78 percent, just as the prior's interval does. The cause is therefore more likely to be the scored rows of this single ledger, or a null that rejects too often, than the prior's fit, and we have not pulled those two apart on this ledger. On the headline archive's forecasts a size check finds that null somewhat liberal: a calibrated forecaster reached percentile 97.6 one time in twenty in the worst case at the estimated correlation, which used equal-mass bins (Section~\ref{sec:calibration-size}). That margin comes from another archive, with other numbers of rows per test, so it is a guide only: the prior's 95.2 here is inside it, and the base rate's 99.8 is above it. The control gives no support to a claim of exact calibration. In the headline archive the prior's rejection is read against the critical value simulated for its own row set and binning (Section~\ref{sec:calibration-size}), so it does not hinge on this margin.

The positive control plants a text signal of known size, and the precedent rung recovers little of it. The control plants a text lift of 1.0 percent between headlines that carry a signal word and headlines that do not, which amounts to a third of the prior standard deviation in the headline-only cell. The planted signal appears in every cell of the ledger, and it makes up a bigger share of the spread in the other two cells, one half and two thirds, because the spreads in those cells are smaller. The headline-only cell is discussed separately below because the plan's first hypothesis, which claims that P2 beats P1 in the headline-only cell, names it and because its scored rows are close in number to the scored rows of the real cell. We set the lift size when we designed the control, before any result of the control existed and after we had seen results from the headline archive, and Table~\ref{tab:positive-control-gain} (Appendix~\ref{app:tables}) lists all the sizes that we ran.

The control uses two designs, and Table~\ref{tab:fmt-control} shows them separately (from Tables~\ref{tab:positive-control}, \ref{tab:positive-control-gain} and~\ref{tab:positive-control-rungs}, Appendix~\ref{app:tables}). The split at the end of the ledger takes a random 15 percent of the tests of a ledger as held out, fits on the remainder as the ledger stands at its end, and repeats this over twelve ledgers drawn under different seeds. Its column for the guard and its gains both come from the fit that leaves out the held-out tests. The second design is the rolling backtest, the design that the real archive receives. We say that the guard \emph{gives a weight} in a cell, or in a pair of origin and cell, when the precedent weight that it selects there is above zero. We express every recovered figure as a share of the gain in log score over P1 of \emph{the told forecaster}, and not as a share of the lift. The told forecaster is P1 fitted with the planted term taken away from each outcome and then given that term back in the forecast for each row, so it is a model told the signal exactly. Gains in log score are in nats (units of log density) per row.
