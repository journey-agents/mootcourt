Can a team predict an A/B test from its history before running it, and what can it use that forecast for? We replay a public archive: 2,949 headline and image tests in seven quarters, each forecast once by models fitted only on earlier tests. History gives a forecast of how the next result will be distributed, better than one that reads no history. A heavy-tailed base rate forecasts it nearly as well as a fitted prior, whose pooled lead of 0.007 nats is not shown once quarters, not tests, are the units. We could not show which test will be significant: the prior gives all tests of one kind and quarter one probability, so its small and fragile skill is a better level and not a ranking, and its probabilities fail a calibration test. We could not show which tests to run: a text classifier orders tests better than a random order matched on arm count, but no model we ran is shown to beat the arm-count rule, which ranks tests by their number of arms, that is, of variants. We could not show which headline drew more clicks: language models playing readers are not shown to beat always picking the earliest headline, nor is an open model asked directly. An embedding classifier is above that rule when tests are resampled and is not shown to be with quarters as the units. All of these models may have seen the archive. Weighting similar past tests gave no gain on the exploratory split, with a method that recovers under a tenth of a planted gain on synthetic data. History says what to expect from a test of its kind and little about which test will succeed. We do not compare a forecast with a live test, and the test still decides what a change did. The evidence is one publisher's archive and untuned models, so a better predictor is not ruled out. Findings are exploratory, except three verdicts that recur on a confirmatory split of 13,822 tests, opened once under rules fixed beforehand in our dated record (no outside registry then). By quarter the prior's lead and its skill against a smoothed base rate are not shown, and the classifier is not shown to rank better than the arm-count rule.
