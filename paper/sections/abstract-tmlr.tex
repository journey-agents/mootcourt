Can a team predict an A/B test from its history before running it, and what does that buy compared with running the test? We replay a public archive: 2,949 headline and image tests, each forecast over seven quarters by models fitted only on earlier tests. The predictor gives a realistic expectation of the result: a heavy-tailed base rate forecasts it nearly as well as a fitted prior, whose lead of 0.007 nats is not distinguished from zero with quarters as units. It did not say which test will be significant: the prior's skill is small and fragile, and its probabilities fail a calibration test. It did not say which tests to run: a text classifier beats a random order matched on arm count, and no model we ran is shown to beat counting arms. It did not say which headline wins: language models playing readers, which may have seen the archive, are not shown to beat picking the earliest, nor is an open model asked directly; an embedding classifier beats that rule, but not once variation between quarters is counted. Weighting similar past tests gave no gain, in a replay that recovers under a tenth of a planted signal. The predictor says what to expect from a test like this one, not whether to ship it; it does not replace the test. The evidence is one publisher's archive and simple models, so a better predictor is not ruled out; it should beat the base rate and counting a test's arms. Findings are exploratory, except that on a confirmatory split of 13,822 tests, opened once under rules fixed beforehand in our dated record (no registry then), three verdicts recur: by quarter the prior's lead and its skill against a smoothed base rate are not shown, nor is ranking above arm count.
