\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Empirical Bayes over many experiments.}
Shrinking parallel estimates toward a common prior \cite{efron1975stein}, random-effects meta-analysis \cite{dersimonian1986meta} and partial pooling \cite{gelman2007multilevel} underlie every rung above P0. In online experimentation the prior has been learned from thousands of historical tests \cite{deng2015objective}, used to separate true effects from lucky draws \cite{azevedo2019empirical}, argued to be heavy-tailed \cite{azevedo2020fat}, fit nonparametrically \cite{guo2020empirical}, and shown on 226 News Feed tests to lower predictive error in a way that would improve launch decisions \cite{coey2019improving}. Two failure modes are documented: selecting on significance distorts estimates \cite{deng2021postselection}, and a historical prior loses calibration when the corpus is winner-selected, pooled across programs or spans heterogeneous metrics \cite{schultzberg2026bayesian}; our admission rules and per-cell priors answer both. P1 is an instance of this family, aimed at a test that has not run rather than at correcting one that has. P0, the program's success rate used as a prior, originates with Kohavi, Deng and Vermeer \cite{kohavi2022intuition}, who tabulate published rates and recommend a posterior probability beside the p-value; we make it cell-specific and score it. Like these models, P1 takes each row's standard error as given. We report that a ratio metric against a noisy reference couples the effect with its own standard error, so scoring at realized precision leaks in-test information into a pre-launch forecast; the remedy is a planned-exposure standard error on a scale whose noise is independent of the reference. The sources above treat corpus composition, not this within-row coupling.

\paragraph{Forecasting before or during a test.}
Surrogate indices \cite{athey2019surrogate}, always-valid inference and confidence sequences \cite{johari2015always,johari2022always,howard2021timeuniform}, Bayesian optional stopping \cite{deng2016continuous} and machine-learned variance reduction \cite{guo2021machine} shorten or sharpen a running test from its own data; our card is issued at time zero. Closer to us are winner predictors on the Upworthy archive: on 17,681 headline tests, embedding or fine-tuned models were only marginally better than chance at naming the winner, a signal then used to steer a bandit \cite{ye2025lola}, and a small model retrained on revealed outcomes picked winners of decisive tests about twice as often as random \cite{clark2026predict}. Our rolling-origin result on text features agrees that the signal is small (Section~\ref{sec:results}). LLM-generated outcomes recover human effects only after calibration against human data \cite{persson2026statistical,ankargren2026when}, and GPT-4 simulations of 70 survey experiments matched pooled human forecasters while overestimating effect sizes \cite{ashokkumar2026large}; our second opinion therefore enters at weight zero.

\paragraph{Human forecasts and win rates.}
Averaged expert forecasts predicted 15 real-effort treatments well, but academic rank and citations did not improve individual accuracy \cite{dellavigna2018predicting}; recording predictions before results are known is now advocated \cite{dellavigna2019predict}; and in a megastudy of 54 exercise interventions, impartial judges failed to predict which worked best \cite{milkman2021megastudies}. P3 therefore freezes the forecast before the result is admitted and earns weight only through out-of-sample skill. Practitioner win rates are the other prior: published success rates run from about a third at Microsoft down to about a tenth at other programs \cite{kohavi2022intuition,kohavi2009controlled}, most ideas are widely reported to fail \cite{kohavi2013online,larsen2022statistical}, and about 18 to 25 percent of results significant at 5 percent are estimated to be nulls \cite{berman2022false}. P0 measures these program-wide rates per cell.

\paragraph{Decision value.}
The launch decision has been cast as a Bayes decision with an explicit loss \cite{goldberg2017decision}, and test-and-roll derives profit-maximizing test sizes far below hypothesis-testing recommendations by trading test exposure to the worse arm against rolling it out \cite{feit2019test}. Single advertising tests can be badly underpowered \cite{lewis2015unfavorable}, which is when past tests matter most; a program optimizing returns should test more ideas with smaller allocations and relaxed thresholds \cite{sudijono2024optimizing}; and the caution that surprising results are usually wrong \cite{kohavi2020trustworthy} becomes explicit abstention in our card. The nearest neighbour to our decision-value evaluation scores launch rules by the cumulative returns they would have produced on 123 historical tests \cite{chou2025evaluating}. Those rules consume each experiment's own data after it runs; ours consumes only the ledger before launch, and we measure prioritization with abstention as test-and-roll regret.

\paragraph{Scoring, calibration and abstention.}
The logarithmic score, Brier score and CRPS are strictly proper \cite{gneiting2007strictly,matheson1976scoring}, forecasts should maximize sharpness subject to calibration \cite{gneiting2007probabilistic}, and the floor we report beside Brier skill is the uncertainty term of Murphy's decomposition \cite{murphy1973vector}. Consistency bars for reliability diagrams \cite{brocker2007increasing} and the pitfalls of binned calibration estimators \cite{vaicenavicius2019evaluating} motivate testing calibration against a perfect-calibration null. Abstention descends from Chow's reject rule \cite{chow1970optimum} and selective classification to a risk target \cite{geifman2017selective}; our triggers reject on support, novelty, reliability and decision instability rather than on posterior probability alone. Interval coverage is a conformal-style validity check \cite{vovk2005algorithmic,angelopoulos2021gentle}; we do not assume exchangeability across cells, hence the novelty trigger.

\paragraph{The two archives.}
The Upworthy Research Archive records every headline test from January 2013 to April 2015 in exploratory, confirmatory and hold-out splits \cite{matias2021upworthy}; our ledger is the exploratory split. Prior analyses explain effects after the fact: text cues causally raise click-through though published advice often gets the direction wrong \cite{banerjee2025language}, each additional negative word raised click-through by about 2.3 percent \cite{robertson2023negativity}, and the archive seeds an agent-based model of information spread \cite{ottaviani2024testing}. The ASOS dataset releases daily checkpoints from 78 real experiments for adaptive-stopping research \cite{liu2021datasets,liu2024statistical}; its documented uses and related adaptive-allocation work \cite{che2024optimization,kim2026odds} concern sequential testing and bandits, not pre-launch prediction.

\paragraph{Local pooling and auxiliary shrinkage.}
The closest prior art to P2 is local empirical Bayes, which borrows strength only from a tailored comparison set, time-aware within an experiment and context-aware across experiments \cite{li2025learning}. P2 differs in that its comparison set is kernel-weighted on text and structured features, shrinks toward the P1 cell prior, carries a leave-one-test-out mixing weight and feeds the abstention triggers, and in that it forecasts before launch rather than stabilizing a running estimate. Stein-type shrinkage combining an experimental estimate with an auxiliary one \cite{rosenman2020combining} is the estimator analogue, with our precedents as the auxiliary source; crossed random effects \cite{baayen2008mixed} extend our one-factor pooling and are left as future work.

\paragraph{What appears new.}
To our knowledge, no prior work issues a pre-launch forecast for a single proposed experiment as a full outcome distribution with a win probability, abstains under named triggers, and scores it against the live result on public archives; the work above corrects estimates after the test, allocates traffic during it, or forecasts without abstention or calibration scoring. We did not find kernel-weighted pooling of past experiments on text and structured features used to forecast a new one, a decision-value evaluation of choosing which experiments to run from pre-launch information alone, a ladder gated so that each rung must beat the one below out of time, or a pre-launch use of the ASOS checkpoints. The nearest neighbours \cite{li2025learning,ye2025lola,clark2026predict} are close enough that we compare against them on matched splits rather than by argument.
