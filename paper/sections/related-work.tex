\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Empirical Bayes over many experiments.}
Shrinking parallel estimates toward a common prior \cite{efron1975stein}, random-effects meta-analysis \cite{dersimonian1986meta} and partial pooling \cite{gelman2007multilevel} underlie the rungs above the base rate. In online experimentation the prior has been learned from thousands of historical tests \cite{deng2015objective}, used to separate true effects from lucky draws \cite{azevedo2019empirical}, argued to be heavy-tailed \cite{azevedo2020fat}, fit nonparametrically \cite{guo2020empirical}, shown to improve launch decisions \cite{coey2019improving}, and combined with an experiment-specific shrinkage factor against the winner's curse \cite{mudd2026breaking}. Selecting on significance distorts estimates \cite{deng2021postselection}, and a historical prior loses calibration when the corpus is winner-selected or pooled across programs \cite{schultzberg2026bayesian}; our admission rules and per-cell priors answer both. The program's success rate as a prior was compiled across programs \cite{kohavi2022intuition,deng2015objective}; P0 makes it cell-specific and scores it, and skill against it follows the forecasting convention of skill against climatology, whose uncertainty term is the floor we report \cite{murphy1973vector}. These models take each row's standard error as given, and the in-test treatment of ratio metrics is the delta method \cite{deng2018delta}; we report a within-row coupling of a ratio metric with its own standard error that neither treats.

\paragraph{Forecasting before or during a test, and screening before it.}
Surrogate indices \cite{athey2019surrogate}, validated as a decision rule \cite{zhang2023evaluating}, always-valid inference \cite{johari2015always,johari2022always,howard2021timeuniform}, Bayesian optional stopping \cite{deng2016continuous} and learned variance reduction \cite{guo2021machine} sharpen a running test from its own data; our card is issued at time zero. Three recent industrial systems screen candidates before a live test: an archive-trained ranker of ad creatives for a many-armed test, validated by offline recall and one live rank \cite{lee2026offline}, a deployed ranker of variants from historical outcomes \cite{hu2026experimentation}, and a back-testing engine that replays campaign data through production allocation code \cite{mazzanti2026backtesting}; none scores an outcome distribution against live results, and none abstains. Winner predictors on the Upworthy archive are the closest text baselines (Table~\ref{tab:fmt-prior-work}): marginally better than chance at naming the winner \cite{ye2025lola}, about twice random on decisive tests after retraining on revealed outcomes \cite{clark2026predict}, and above chance in domain, chance out of it \cite{khimani2026finetuning}.

Our pairwise TF-IDF classifier re-implements the simplest member of that family on matched splits and reaches 0.601 on the decisive rows (Section~\ref{sec:ladder-results}); the published models were not rerun. The three challengers are the simplest members of their families in their scikit-learn implementations \cite{pedregosa2011scikit}: gradient boosting \cite{friedman2001greedy}, TF-IDF term weighting \cite{salton1988term} with logistic regression, and the local comparison set after \cite{li2025learning}.
Simulators that stand in for the audience report direction only \cite{li2026simgym,hut2026can}; outcomes generated by large language models (LLMs) recover human effects only after calibration against human data \cite{persson2026statistical,ankargren2026when,ashokkumar2026large}, persona-prompted models drift \cite{bisbee2024synthetic}, and respondent grounding buys fidelity on self-report, not on live experiments \cite{park2024llm}; our second opinion therefore enters at weight zero. A trial-level surrogacy gate on Upworthy interventions sends every one to a live test \cite{schultzberg2026when}, the bar our gates are the analogue of.

\paragraph{Human forecasts, win rates and decision value.}
Expert forecasts of treatment effects help when averaged \cite{dellavigna2018predicting}, should be recorded before results are known \cite{dellavigna2019predict}, and failed to pick the best intervention in a megastudy \cite{milkman2021megastudies}; P3 therefore freezes the forecast before the result is admitted. Published success rates run from about a third to about a tenth of ideas \cite{kohavi2022intuition,kohavi2009controlled,kohavi2013online,larsen2022statistical}, and a share of significant results is estimated to be nulls \cite{berman2022false}, descending from the false-positive report probability \cite{wacholder2004assessing} and the low-prior regime \cite{ioannidis2005why}; P0 measures these rates per cell. The launch decision has been cast as a Bayes decision \cite{goldberg2017decision}, test-and-roll sizes tests from the cost of exposure and rollout \cite{feit2019test}, single tests are often underpowered \cite{lewis2015unfavorable}, a program should test more ideas at smaller allocations \cite{sudijono2024optimizing}, and the caution that surprising results are usually wrong \cite{kohavi2020trustworthy} becomes abstention here. The card's significance probability is an assurance in the sense of \cite{ohagan2005assurance}, a success probability averaged over the prior rather than a power at one assumed effect. The nearest decision-value evaluation scores launch rules by the returns they would have produced on historical tests \cite{chou2025evaluating}; those rules read each experiment's own data, ours only the ledger, and our oracle, the best positive observed lift among a test's arms, carries the winner's curse \cite{lee2018winners} and is an inflated upper bound.

\paragraph{Scoring, calibration, abstention and the protocol.}
The logarithmic score (log score), Brier score and continuous ranked probability score (CRPS) are strictly proper \cite{gneiting2007strictly,matheson1976scoring}, forecasts should maximize sharpness subject to calibration \cite{gneiting2007probabilistic}, and the rolling origin is the forecasting literature's out-of-sample protocol \cite{tashman2000outofsample}, applied here to versioned ledger snapshots; leakage is used in the sense of \cite{kaufman2012leakage}, and the paired, clustered log-score comparisons behind the admission guard and the guarded-against-unguarded reading are tests of equal predictive accuracy in the line of \cite{diebold1995comparing,giacomini2006tests}, read as sizing rules. Consistency bars for reliability diagrams \cite{brocker2007increasing} and the pitfalls of binned calibration estimators \cite{vaicenavicius2019evaluating} motivate the clustered null and the equal-mass estimator of the expected calibration error (ECE). Abstention descends from Chow's reject rule \cite{chow1970optimum} and selective classification \cite{geifman2017selective}; a recent multimodal judge of winners reaches a kappa on real tests and only a vote-margin gate that abstains on half of them lifts it \cite{dooskin2026judge}; our triggers extend the gate to support, novelty, reliability and decision instability. Interval coverage is a conformal-style validity check \cite{vovk2005algorithmic,angelopoulos2021gentle} without exchangeability across cells, hence the novelty trigger.

\paragraph{The two archives, local pooling, and what appears new.}
The Upworthy Research Archive records every headline test from January 2013 to April 2015 in exploratory, confirmatory and hold-out splits \cite{matias2021upworthy}; prior analyses explain its effects after the fact \cite{banerjee2025language,robertson2023negativity,ottaviani2024testing}. The ASOS dataset releases daily checkpoints from 78 real experiments \cite{liu2021datasets,liu2024statistical}; its documented uses \cite{che2024optimization,kim2026odds} concern sequential testing and bandits. The closest prior art to P2 is local empirical Bayes \cite{li2025learning}; Stein-type shrinkage with an auxiliary estimate \cite{rosenman2020combining} and crossed random effects \cite{baayen2008mixed} are the estimator analogues. The claim that survives the survey is narrow: no prior system issues a pre-launch outcome distribution with a win probability for a single proposed experiment, abstains under named triggers, and scores it with proper scores, gates and a leakage audit against live results on public archives. The nearest neighbours \cite{li2025learning,ye2025lola,clark2026predict,khimani2026finetuning} are represented on the matched splits by re-implementations of their simplest members (a local comparison set and a TF-IDF classifier), not by the published systems, so the negative on text is a statement about the TF-IDF and singular value decomposition (SVD) space.
